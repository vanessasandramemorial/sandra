---
title: "Obituary"
---

Sandra Ivelisse Aponte Santiago passed away on September 22, 2026, in Guaynabo, Puerto Rico, after a battle with cancer. She was 73. She leaves behind a family who loved her, friends all over the world, and many former students whose futures are brighter because she believed in them.

The third of four sisters, Sandra was born in San Juan on October 7, 1952, and grew up in Puerto Rico. After graduating cum laude in sociology from the University of Puerto Rico, she set out for Boston.

There she helped immigrant families find homes and work, then taught in the bilingual program at the James P. Timilty Middle School while earning her master's degree in education, counseling, and curriculum development. As a teacher, she made her students feel welcome and helped them take pride in their own cultures. She married in Boston and welcomed her first daughter there.

Throughout her career, she traveled far from home and back again. In Venezuela, she taught sociology at the Universidad Rafael Urdaneta and, at the Centro Venezolano Americano del Zulia, guided young Venezuelans who dreamed of studying in the United States. Back home in Puerto Rico, she brought concerts, festivals, and conferences to the Universidad del Sagrado Corazón. In 1987 the family moved to Hamden, Connecticut, where her second daughter was born.

Wherever she went, Sandra was helping people find their way, and nowhere more than in the schools of New Haven, where as a guidance counselor she did some of her most meaningful work. She helped her students through the struggles of each day, and she helped them see, and reach for, brighter futures. Many of them applied to college with Sandra by their side.

In 2013 she retired and came home to Puerto Rico, where she spent the rest of her life. In retirement she began taking painting classes, and created several beautiful paintings, which you can see in the [Art](/artifacts) section of this site.

Sandra loved to travel, and she met the world with an open heart. She saw Machu Picchu and Egypt; Turkey, a trip she was especially excited about; Spain, Italy, and Switzerland; Cuba, Colombia, and Venezuela; Mexico and Canada; and all across the United States.

She was a deeply spiritual person, and read widely about spiritual topics. She loved going to the theater and sharing that experience with loved ones. She had a lifelong passion for telenovelas. And music filled her days, from Rubén Blades to Rocío Dúrcal to Celine Dion and many others. But nothing mattered more to her than the people she loved. She treasured her time with family and friends, and spent hours on the phone keeping in touch with everyone she cared about, near and far.

She is survived by her daughters, Vanessa and Viviana; her four grandchildren; and her sisters, Awilda and Sheila. She was preceded in death by her sister Ivette.

Her warmth, her curiosity, and her faith in others live on in everyone she touched.

A private service will be held for her family.
